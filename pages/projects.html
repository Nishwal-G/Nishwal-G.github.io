Research portfolio

Current work in biological physics and active matter.

My present interests centre on collective behaviour in living systems: how active components coordinate, how chromatin organisation shapes transcription, and how biological systems store information. I use theoretical and computational approaches to connect microscopic interactions with emergent dynamics.

Earlier in my training, I worked extensively on quantum magnetism and neutron-scattering studies of frustrated materials. Those projects remain an important part of my research background, but they are not my current focus.

Ongoing since August 2025 Chromatin physics

Chromatin clustering and its role in transcription

With Davide Marenduzzo

This project asks whether chromatin-folding states associated with transcription factories can act as stable cellular memories. It connects physical models of genome organisation with the Waddington Landscape to explore how cellular identity may be preserved and restored.

MPhys project · Computational biophysics · 2025–2026

Waddington’s landscape through transcriptional dynamics

Using a coarse-grained polymer model, this project investigated how chromatin organisation and transcription-factor dynamics shape gene expression, differentiation, and cellular memory. Telegraph-model analysis connected simulated transcription traces to switching, bursting, and the stabilisation of cellular states.

Completed research · Active matter

Modelling bacterial turbulence with finite tumbling

With Alexander Morozov

This project examined how finite tumbling duration changes the onset of hydrodynamic instability in suspensions of bacterial swimmers. Coupled Smoluchowski equations and linear stability analysis linked microscopic run-and-tumble dynamics to emergent large-scale flows.

  • Linear stability
  • Smoluchowski equations
  • Spherical harmonics
  • Active suspensions
